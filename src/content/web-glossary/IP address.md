---
description: A numeric address that identifies a device on a network, such as 93.184.216.34.
---

Every device on the [internet](./Internet.md) has an address so data knows where to go. Older IPv4 addresses look like 203.0.113.42; newer IPv6 addresses are much longer, like 2001:db8::1, because the world ran out of the short kind.

People rarely deal with IP addresses directly. You type a [domain name](./Domain%20name.md), and the [Domain Name System](./Domain%20Name%20System%20%28DNS%29.md) looks up the matching IP address for you — like a contact list turning a name into a phone number.

Your home network usually shares one public IP address given by your [internet service provider](./Internet%20service%20provider%20%28ISP%29.md). Websites see that address, which gives them a rough idea of where you are.

_Avoid:_ treating an IP address as a person's identity — many people can share one, and it changes often.

_Usage:_

"Can we block that spammer by their IP address?"

"We can try, but they'll probably get a new one. And we might block a whole office or café sharing it."
