---
description: Software whose source code is published for anyone to use, study, change and share.
---

Open source projects publish their code, usually on a [version control](./Version%20control.md) platform, under a license that lets others use and adapt it. Much of the web runs on open source: popular [browser](./Browser.md) engines, [web servers](./Web%20server.md), [databases](./Database.md), [frameworks](./Framework.md) and [bundlers](./Bundler.md).

Anyone can report bugs, suggest fixes or copy ("fork") a project to take it in a new direction. Projects are maintained by volunteers, companies, or both.

"Free to use" doesn't mean "no rules". Licenses differ — some require you to share your own changes, or to credit the authors — and every project you depend on needs someone to keep it updated.

_Avoid:_ assuming open source means "no license" — every project has terms; read them before shipping.

_Usage:_

"Can we use this library in our paid product?"

"Check its license first. Most allow it, but a few require us to publish our code too."
