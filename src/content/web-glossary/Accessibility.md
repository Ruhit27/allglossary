description: Making websites usable by everyone, including people with disabilities.
---

Often shortened to a11y. An accessible [website](./Website.md) works for people who are blind and use screen readers, who can't use a mouse, who have low vision or color blindness, who are deaf, or who have cognitive differences. It also helps everyone else — captions help in a noisy train; good contrast helps in bright sunlight.

Much of it comes down to well-built [HTML](./HTML.md): real headings, buttons and labels, image descriptions ("alt text"), and clear [hyperlink](./Hyperlink.md) text. [Forms](./Form.md) need labels and readable errors; everything must work with a keyboard alone; text must contrast with its background. [Responsive design](./Responsive%20design.md) also helps people who zoom in.

In many countries accessibility is a legal requirement, following a widely used standard called WCAG.

_Avoid:_ "accessibility" as only for blind people — it also covers keyboard users, deaf people, low vision and cognitive differences.

_Usage:_

"The new menu looks great."

"It doesn't open with the keyboard, though. Someone who can't use a mouse can't navigate the site."
