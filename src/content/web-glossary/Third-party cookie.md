---
description: A cookie set by a different company than the site you're visiting, often an advertiser.
---

When a [web page](./Web%20page.md) includes content from other companies — an ad, a "like" button, an analytics script — those companies can set their own [cookies](./Cookie.md) in your [browser](./Browser.md). Because the same company's content appears on thousands of sites, its cookie lets it recognize you across all of them.

That's the classic machinery of cross-site [tracking](./Tracking.md): an ad network learns you looked at running shoes on one site and shows you shoe ads on another. Privacy laws require sites to ask before using them, hence the [consent banner](./Consent%20banner.md).

Browsers increasingly block third-party cookies by default. Safari and Firefox already do, which has pushed advertisers toward other methods.

_Avoid:_ "third-party" meaning dangerous or malicious — it just means the cookie belongs to someone other than the site you're on.

_Usage:_

"Our ad conversion numbers dropped on Safari."

"Safari blocks third-party cookies, so the ad network can't link the click to the purchase anymore."
