---
description: Recording what people do online, on one site or across many, usually for analytics or advertising.
---

Tracking ranges from simple to invasive. A site counting its own visitors and which pages they view — analytics — is tracking, and helps it improve. Following one person across many sites to build a profile of their interests is also tracking, mostly for targeted advertising.

Cross-site tracking has traditionally relied on [third-party cookies](./Third-party%20cookie.md), plus techniques like invisible images and "fingerprinting" (identifying a [browser](./Browser.md) by its unique combination of settings). Your [IP address](./IP%20address.md) gives away rough location as well.

Privacy laws like the EU's GDPR require clear consent for most tracking, which is why sites show a [consent banner](./Consent%20banner.md). Browsers also block more of it every year.

_Avoid:_ "tracking" as only advertising — counting visits to improve your own site is tracking too, and privacy laws may still apply.

_Usage:_

"Can we add the ad pixel to the checkout page?"

"Only if users have accepted marketing cookies in the consent banner."
