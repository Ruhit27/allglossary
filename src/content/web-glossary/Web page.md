---
description: A single document on the web, with its own URL, that a browser displays.
---

One document you see in your [browser](./Browser.md): an article, a product page, a search results page. Each web page has its own [URL](./URL.md). Under the hood it's an [HTML](./HTML.md) file, styled with [CSS](./CSS.md) and often made interactive with [JavaScript](./JavaScript.md), plus the images, fonts and videos it pulls in.

When you open a page, the browser downloads the HTML, reads it, fetches everything else it mentions, and draws the result on screen. Many pages together, under one name, make a [website](./Website.md).

A page can be the same for everyone, or built fresh for each visitor — your inbox page shows different emails than someone else's even though the URL looks the same.

_Avoid:_ "page" for a whole site — say which page, since each one has its own address.

_Usage:_

"The page is broken."

"Which one? The home page loads fine for me, so send me the exact URL you're on."
