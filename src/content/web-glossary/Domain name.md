---
description: The human-readable name of a website, such as example.com, that points to its server.
---

The readable name people type instead of a numeric [IP address](./IP%20address.md): wikipedia.org, lemonde.fr, example.com. Domains are read right to left. The last part (.com, .org, .uk) is the top-level domain; the part before it is the name someone registered; anything further left, like shop.example.com, is a subdomain the owner can create freely.

You don't buy a domain forever — you rent it, usually yearly, from a [domain registrar](./Domain%20registrar.md). The [Domain Name System](./Domain%20Name%20System%20%28DNS%29.md) then connects the name to the address of your [hosting](./Hosting.md).

A domain is the stable part of a [URL](./URL.md): you can move your site to a new server, and as long as the domain points to it, visitors won't notice.

_Avoid:_ "domain" for the website itself — the domain is just the name; the site lives on a server elsewhere.

_Usage:_

"Our domain renewal email went to someone who left."

"Fix that today. If the domain expires, the site and our email both stop working."
